# Phase 1 - Discovery Questions

The full Phase 1 question batch, with branching logic. Ask in **one consolidated turn**, branched by what the user already gave you. Never re-ask information already provided.

---

## Branching Logic - What to Skip

| If the user already provided | Skip |
|---|---|
| Destination ("trip to Tokyo") | The destination question |
| Specific dates ("July 10–24") | The dates question |
| Trip type that implies tier ("honeymoon") | Confirm tier rather than re-ask |
| Travelers ("for me and my wife") | Travelers count question (still ask ages/dietary if relevant) |
| Budget envelope ("under $5K total") | The budget question (confirm what's included) |
| Pace explicitly ("we want to relax", "we want to pack it in") | The pace question |
| Passion explicitly ("food trip", "art-focused") | The passion-rank question (still ask for #2 and non-negotiables) |

If they gave half-information, ask only for the missing half.

---

## The Consolidated Discovery Batch

Lead with **the inspiration question.** It surfaces motive and reveals the passion better than any logistics question. Then go through the rest in one batch.

```
Before I dig into options - quick discovery batch so I can build a trip
that's actually yours.

What inspired this trip - what's the feeling or moment you want to
walk away with?

Then the logistics:

1. Home airport or city (so we can price flights and weigh transit)
2. Dates and total length - firm dates, flexible window, or open?
3. Travelers - how many, ages, mobility, dietary needs, language comfort
4. Budget envelope - total or per-person-per-day, and what's IN it
   (flights? everything? on-the-ground only?)
5. Travel-style tier - budget / mid / upscale / luxury
6. Pace - packed / balanced / chill
7. Passions ranked - top 2 or 3 from: food, art, history, nature,
   photography, adrenaline, rest, nightlife, design, wellness,
   wildlife, architecture, music, family connection
8. Non-negotiables - must do/see, AND must avoid
9. Hard constraints - work obligations, religious observances,
   allergies, jet-lag tolerance, anything specific I should know

Anything else floating around in your head about the trip - answer all
of it in one message. I'll come back with a shape.
```

---

## Branch Variations

### If Destination Already Given

Drop "where" entirely. Reframe the inspiration question around feeling:

> "What's the feeling you want to walk away with from [destination] - and what's drawing you to it now?"

### If Dates Already Given

Drop the dates question. Add this to logistics:

> "Are those dates firm, or can they shift by a week or two if it meaningfully improves the trip?"

### If Trip Type Implies Tier (honeymoon, anniversary, milestone birthday)

Drop the tier question. Confirm framing:

> "I'll plan this at the upscale tier - splurge moments are part of the point. Tell me if you want to dial it down or push higher."

### If Couple or Partner Trip

Add a single question to the batch:

> "Is one of you the planner and the other along for the ride, or do you both want input on the shape?"

### If Family With Kids

Add ages, dietary, mobility, and:

> "What's the kids' tolerance for: long flights, long walks, late dinners, museum days?"

### If Solo

Add:

> "Solo trip energy - recharge, adventure, social, or build-your-own?"

### If Group of Friends

Add:

> "Who's the decision-maker if there's a tie? Anyone with a stronger constraint than the rest (budget, mobility, dietary)?"

### If Already Said "Bucket List"

Add:

> "What's the one thing on this trip that, if it didn't happen, the trip would feel incomplete?"

## Why This Order

The inspiration question goes first because **vision before logistics** earns trust and surfaces the passion-point that becomes the lens. (See `elite-planner-principles.md` §11.)

The logistics block is asked all-at-once because the alternative - drip-feeding questions - burns trust and wall-clock time. (See `elite-planner-principles.md` §1, the WOW Pledge norms.)

Budget sits in the middle of the batch, never first. Asking budget first is the cardinal discovery-call mistake.

Non-negotiables ask both sides - the must-do AND the must-avoid. The must-avoid list is often more useful than the must-do.

Hard constraints last because the user often forgets these until prompted, and the prompt is what surfaces the deal-breakers (mobility, religious observance, allergies).

---

## After the User Answers

1. Confirm what you heard back to them in one paragraph - destination(s), dates, travelers, the headline passion, the budget tier, the pace.
2. Flag any contradiction you noticed (they said "chill" but listed eight passions, or they said "luxury" but a budget that doesn't support it). Push back gently with the math.
3. Move to Phase 2 (if destination undecided) or Phase 3 (logistics scaffold).

Do not start writing the day-by-day before this echo-back. The echo-back is the alignment moment - and where surface-level requirements often get corrected.
