# Elite Planner Principles - Research Synthesis

These are the principles encoded into the `travel-agent` skill, drawn from how the world's most respected travel planners actually work. Each principle is attributed so a reader can verify.

---

## 1. Connections and Current Intel Beat Encyclopedic Recall

**Source: Wendy Perrin, the WOW List**
The WOW List is a curated roster of "trip-planning experts" chosen because they are "exceptionally knowledgeable and well-connected" and can "open doors that others can't" - including handling clients out of unforeseen jams. Trip planners on the list sign a pledge that includes a 48-hour response window and an in-depth design call (phone or video) before any planning begins.

**Why it matters for the agent:** The general-purpose internet is full of stale information. Booking aggregators don't know about a museum's six-month renovation, a chef's relocation, or a ferry strike. **Web search before every concrete claim.** Treat your stored knowledge as a starting hypothesis, not a fact.

> Source: [The 2026 WOW List, Wendy Perrin](https://www.wendyperrin.com/wow-list/), [WOW List FAQs](https://www.wendyperrin.com/plan-trip-faq/)

---

## 2. It's Not Where You Want To Go - It's How You Want To Feel

**Source: Black Tomato**
Black Tomato's founding philosophy: "It's not where you want to go; it's how you want to feel." Every journey begins with what they call an emotional diagnostic - a 90-minute conversation that maps "themes from childhood nostalgia to aspirational moods" before any destination talk. They describe their work as "cinematic in scope, intimate in execution."

**Why it matters for the agent:** Discovery is unhurried and emotional first, logistical second. Ask what feeling the traveler is chasing before asking which city. The feeling is the lens; the destination is the canvas.

> Source: [Black Tomato Redefines Luxury Travel with Emotionally Resonant Journeys, Worth](https://worth.com/tom-marchant-black-tomato-luxury-travel/), [Black Tomato About](https://www.blacktomato.com/us/about-us/)

---

## 3. The Passion Is the Lens for the Whole Trip

**Source: Indagare**
Indagare matches every client to a Trip Designer "who understands your personal passions and travel goals." Their flagship Indagare Journeys product is built around a passion ("design," "food," and so on) rather than a place - the same destination is rebuilt around what the traveler cares about.

**Why it matters for the agent:** A food traveler in Rome gets one trip. An art traveler in Rome gets a different trip. Same city, different itinerary. The passion-point shapes neighborhood, hotel, day order, and reservations - not just one "food day" tacked on.

> Source: [Indagare Travel](https://indagare.com/), [Indagare FAQ](https://indagare.com/faq)

---

## 4. Three Positioned Options Beat a Top-10 List

**Source: Black Tomato, FlyOver, and the broader bespoke-travel field**
Bespoke planners do not hand clients a menu. They narrow ruthlessly, present a small number of options, and **state the position** - why this over that - out loud. The reason is partly cognitive (a top-10 paralyzes) and partly trust (a planner who refuses to choose isn't really planning).

**Why it matters for the agent:** When the user asks "where should we go," answer with three real options, one paragraph each, with the tradeoff stated. Never neutral both-sides framing. Pick a position.

> Source: [Black Tomato Review, Men's Journal](https://www.mensjournal.com/travel/black-tomato-review)

---

## 5. Pacing Wins. Fewer, Deeper Experiences Beat Packed Days

**Source: Conventional wisdom across Wendy Perrin, Indagare, Black Tomato, Virtuoso advisors**
The most cited mistake in trip planning, across every elite source: cramming. Two great days beat four exhausting ones. The traveler who saw Paris's Louvre, Versailles, the Eiffel Tower, Sainte-Chapelle, and the Catacombs in one day saw nothing. Build in true rest. Build in a chill block every day.

**Why it matters for the agent:** Resist the urge to fill every block. Day 1 is jet-lag day. Travel days are travel days. One signature anchor per day plus a weather/energy backup is usually enough.

> Source: [How to Plan a Trip Itinerary Everyone Will Enjoy, Wendy Perrin](https://www.wendyperrin.com/how-to-plan-a-trip-itinerary/)

---

## 6. Anchor Each Day With One Signature Experience Plus a Backup

**Source: Field practice across luxury concierges and bespoke planners**
The day has a spine: one experience that earns the slot. The rest of the day flows from it. The backup catches weather, energy crashes, and closures without derailing the day.

**Why it matters for the agent:** Every day-by-day entry needs a *named* anchor, the reason it earns the slot, and a *named* backup. "Find a museum if it rains" is not a plan.

---

## 7. End-to-End Thinking - Every Transfer, Every Layover

**Source: Wendy Perrin's "local fixer" mandate**
The most-cited reason WOW List planners earn their fees is that they handle the messy connective tissue: airport transfers, late check-ins, missed connections, chef no-shows, weather pivots. Trip-planners worth the title plan the flat-tire-on-the-runway scenario.

**Why it matters for the agent:** Surface contingencies before they bite. Where is the airport relative to the hotel? What if the train strikes? Who's on call if something breaks at 11 PM? The skill flags these in the prep checklist and reservations tracker.

> Source: [The Secret to Extraordinary Travel, Wendy Perrin](https://www.wendyperrin.com/the-secret-to-extraordinary-travel-heres-proof-that-the-wow-list-works/)

---

## 8. Anticipate, Do Not React

**Source: Discovery-call best practices, luxury advisor field guides**
Top advisors flag what needs advance action - visas (some take 60+ days), vaccinations (yellow fever needs 10 days lead time, Japanese encephalitis needs longer), restaurant lottery windows, train pass purchase deadlines, cherry-blossom-season hotel bookings (often nine months out).

**Why it matters for the agent:** The reservations tracker and the prep checklist exist for this. If a thing has a deadline, surface it with the deadline date computed for *this* trip's dates.

> Source: [How to Conduct the Perfect Client Discovery Call, Riveting Trips](https://rivetingtrips.com/how-to-conduct-travel-advisor-discovery-call/)

---

## 9. Surprise and Delight Inside the Budget

**Source: Universal across Indagare, Black Tomato, and Virtuoso advisors**
The trip everyone remembers includes one moment that wasn't on the spreadsheet - the pre-opening museum walkthrough, the chef's-table seating that wasn't on the menu, the dawn safari window the standard tour skips, the rooftop your hotel concierge unlocks. It does not have to be expensive. It has to be *thoughtful* and *tied to the stated interest.*

**Why it matters for the agent:** One delight per trip, tied to the traveler's #1 passion. The art lover gets the after-hours museum tip. The wildlife traveler gets the dawn window. The food lover gets the market-then-cooking-class loop.

---

## 10. Push Back on Bad Ideas - With a Better Alternative

**Source: The discovery call best practices canon**
Riveting Trips, Travel Industry Solutions, and the broader luxury-advisor literature converge: the advisor's job includes saying *no* - to twelve cities in ten days, to a sightseeing day on a jet-lag arrival, to dinner reservations at chains in a food city, to peak-season Tokyo with no Hanami strategy. The "no" must come paired with a "here's what to do instead."

**Why it matters for the agent:** Silent compliance with a self-inflicted-wound itinerary is a failure of duty. The skill includes a pushback table for the most common bad ideas. After one pushback, if the user insists, the agent complies but documents the tradeoff.

> Source: [How To Effectively Qualify Clients And Understand Their Travel Needs, Vincent Vacations](https://www.vincentvacations.com/how-to-travel-agent/how-to-effectively-qualify-clients-and-understand-their-travel-needs)

---

## 11. Lead With Value and Vision, Not Price

**Source: Riveting Trips discovery-call methodology**
The advisor who opens a discovery call by asking the budget loses the room. The opening question is "what inspired this trip?" or "what did you love about the last trip you took?" Vision before money. Once the traveler has shared what they're chasing, the budget conversation lands as a constraint to design within, not a cap to negotiate down to.

**Why it matters for the agent:** Phase 1 leads with the inspiration question. Budget is asked, but it sits inside the batch - never first.

> Source: [How to Conduct the Perfect Client Discovery Call, Riveting Trips](https://rivetingtrips.com/how-to-conduct-travel-advisor-discovery-call/)

---

## 12. Active Listening - Verbal AND Non-Verbal

**Source: MasterAdvisor / Travel Market Report**
Even on a video call, advisors watch for non-verbal cues: tone shifts, what the client *understates*, what they keep coming back to, what they wave away too quickly. In chat, the equivalent is paying attention to what the user emphasizes and what they leave underspecified - that gap is often where the real preference lives.

**Why it matters for the agent:** When the user says "yeah, food's fine, whatever" but later mentions three restaurants by name, the passion is food and they're being modest. Catch it. Lean into it.

> Source: [MasterAdvisor 73: Tips for Qualifying Luxury Clients, Travel Market Report](http://www.travelmarketreport.com/Training/articles/MasterAdvisor-73-Tips-for-Qualifying-Luxury-Clients)

---

## 13. The 48-Hour Responsiveness Norm

**Source: WOW List Pledge, Wendy Perrin**
WOW List planners pledge to respond to inquiries within 48 business hours. The norm exists because trip planning has windows - flight prices move, hotels sell out, advance reservations close. Slow response is a planning failure.

**Why it matters for the agent:** When the user asks a follow-up, answer with substance. When you flag a deadline, flag it loudly. The skill treats deadlines as first-class objects in the reservations tracker.

> Source: [WOW List FAQs, Wendy Perrin](https://www.wendyperrin.com/plan-trip-faq/)
